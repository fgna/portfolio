Vibe Coding Projects

I build software because I want to use it myself.

My projects usually start with a concrete need in everyday life. Something takes unnecessary time, creates mental load, gives me too little control, or works differently from the way that fits my life. With AI, the threshold for turning that need into my own software has dropped dramatically. An idea can become something I actually use within a short time.

Why I do this

Vibe coding is DIY for the digital age.

Software has largely been shaped by professional development teams. Vibe coding opens up a more personal path: I can build digital tools myself and shape them around my everyday life, interests and ideas.

I value the independence this creates. I want control over my data and the freedom to shape digital tools around the way I live and work.

Building itself matters just as much. Vibe coding is a form of deliberate play for me: experimenting, understanding, changing things and learning along the way. I can explore technologies I previously knew little about and follow ideas whose outcome is open at the start.

There is also the simple joy of making. Having an idea and shortly afterwards seeing something working on my phone is a major part of the motivation.

FreyaOS

Keep fewer things in my head.

Tasks, appointments, routines, contacts and longer-term plans constantly create small signals about what matters and what deserves attention next. The mental work lies in bringing all of that information together and drawing the right conclusions at the right moment.

FreyaOS takes over part of that work. It combines signals from different parts of my life and turns them into a useful view of today, the coming week and longer-term plans. The personal name is intentional. FreyaOS is shaped around my life, habits and way of planning.

The idea behind it: A personal organization system can reduce mental load by recognizing connections and bringing information together at the right moment. The system adapts to its user.

PocketDev

Use my daily commute to keep working on my software projects.

I spend part of every workday on a bus. I want to use that time to work on my apps. My development machine is at home, while my phone is already in my hand.

PocketDev makes the important development workflows accessible directly from the phone. I can switch projects, follow changes, start tests and builds, install apps and control running processes.

The idea behind it: My development workflow should fit my everyday life. The lower the barriers between an idea and the next step, the easier it becomes to use my time for developing, experimenting and learning.

Personal Library

See my book collection digitally and rediscover what is on my shelves.

With more than 600 books, I want to know what is actually sitting on my shelves. Personal Library makes the collection searchable and lets me filter, group and rediscover titles that have slipped out of view.

Adding a book is deliberately simple: I take a photo directly in the app. Title and author are recognized, and additional information is enriched from publicly available internet sources. I can then review the entry and add it to my library.

The idea behind it: My digital library should create a new way to explore the books I already own. Easy capture keeps the collection current, while search, filters and grouping help me explore the collection and rediscover books.

Android LLM Service

Make local AI a shared capability across my own apps.

FreyaOS and Personal Library both began using local language models for different tasks. That created a need for shared AI infrastructure on the phone.

Android LLM Service provides local apps with access to one shared model. FreyaOS can use it for assistant functions, while Personal Library can use the same infrastructure to recognize books from photos.

The idea behind it: Shared capabilities across my apps can become personal infrastructure. The project is also a playground for understanding how local AI can work usefully on everyday hardware.

Health Data Hub

Own my health data and explore it freely.

Wearables collect highly personal information about movement, sleep and other aspects of the body over many years. I want to store that data on my own hardware and retain long-term control over it.

Health Data Hub brings health data into my own local database, backs it up and makes it recoverable. This creates a long-term personal history that can support my own analyses.

The idea behind it: Data sovereignty also means being able to work with my own data. A personal health database supports analyses and connections shaped around my questions and extends the range of views provided by individual vendor apps.

GoodVibes

Use connected hardware in a way that fits my own usage patterns.

GoodVibes grew from a desire for more control over the way a connected device is operated. The custom Android app communicates directly with the hardware and enables individually designed workflows.

Several devices can be controlled together or individually, custom patterns can be saved and favorited, and the interaction model can evolve with actual use.

The idea behind it: Controlling the hardware interface myself gives me control over the experience as well. I especially enjoy the immediate connection between software and the physical world: a change in code changes the behavior of a real device.

PhotoMap

Bring travel photos back into the context where they were created.

A trip can leave hundreds of photos behind. Their geographic relationship tells part of the story of the journey and adds a layer that a classic gallery barely reveals.

PhotoMap uses the location data in the images to create an interactive map. Trips can be explored geographically, with photos appearing directly at the places where they were taken.

The idea behind it: Photos are part of a journey and a place. The map restores that context and creates another way to rediscover past trips.

How I work

From a need to an experiment and onward.

Many projects start with something that occupies, interests or intrigues me. That gives me direct feedback about the value an idea creates in everyday life.

01

Build something real quickly

I aim for a working version early. A functioning prototype creates immediate feedback and quickly leads to better questions.

02

Use it and evolve it

Many decisions emerge from using my own apps. Everyday life shows which workflows matter and where further possibilities appear.

03

Play and learn

Some projects start mainly with curiosity: Can a local language model recognize a book from a photo? How can an Android app directly control physical hardware? What becomes possible with a long-term personal health database?

04

Deliberate play

This deliberate play is a central part of vibe coding for me. It creates knowledge, new ideas and sometimes a tool that becomes a lasting part of my everyday life.

An evolving portfolio

Ideas become things I can use.

Some of these projects already solve the need they were built for reliably. Others keep evolving as I use them, learn something new or explore another idea.

That is what interests me about vibe coding: the distance between “Surely this should be possible” and “I built it” has become very small.

Software becomes a form of making: a material I can use to solve problems, experiment, learn and shape things that previously existed only as ideas.
